**Topic: “Tang Ping” (Lying Flat) among Chinese Youth**

*Search logic: broad topic → keyword refinement → paper screening → final selection*

# 1. Initial research interest

**Initial topic statement:** Why do Chinese young people choose “Tang Ping” (lying flat) rather than continued hard work under intense competition, and how is this behavior socially evaluated?

**Preliminary focus:** The search focuses on the antecedents and mechanisms of lying flat (e.g., competition, pressure, effort, withdrawal) and the consequences or evaluations associated with the behavior.

The search was conducted iteratively. Broad keywords were used first; terms found in titles, abstracts, and author keywords were then added to the refined search. This avoids constructing the keywords around only the two final papers.

# 5. Why the final two papers were selected

Both papers are directly centered on Tang Ping / lying flat rather than treating it only as a secondary variable.

Hsu (2022) explains the external/social-evaluation side: when return expectation is low, lying flat becomes more acceptable even though effort-making remains positively evaluated.

Qi and Huang (2026) explains the internal/process side: high-achieving students withdraw from hard work under social pressure, emotional fatigue, resource imbalance, and perceived futility.

The methods complement each other: Hsu uses a quantitative experiment, while Qi and Huang use qualitative in-depth interviews.

Together, the pair can answer a broader question: why lying flat emerges under competitive pressure and low perceived returns, and under what conditions it becomes socially understandable.
